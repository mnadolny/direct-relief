46:1

46 to 1. About $46 in medicine reaches health providers for every $1 it costs to deliver it.

Manufacturers donate the medicine. Donors fund the warehouse, the cold chain and the flights that get it where it's requested. That's why a gift to Direct Relief moves so much.

Five evaluators, five different questions

Charity Navigator looks beyond finances. CharityWatch grades cash efficiency. Forbes publishes ratios. BBB tests 20 standards. Candid rates openness. Here's what each one asks, and how Direct Relief answers.

Charity Navigator100 / 100

Is the charity effective, accountable and financially healthy?

A weighted 0–100 score, translated into zero to four stars. Covers impact and measurement, accountability, leadership, planning, workplace policies and compensation.

CharityWatchA+

How efficiently does it use cash donations?

An A+ to F grade from adjusted program spending and fundraising cost. Excess reserves can lower the grade. FY2024: 90% to programs, $5 to raise $100.

Forbes99%

How much of each donation goes to the mission?

Publishes charitable commitment, fundraising efficiency and donor dependency ratios. 2025: 99% charitable commitment, 100% fundraising efficiency (rounded).

BBB Wise Giving Alliance20 of 20

Does it meet accountability standards?

Pass or fail on 20 standards covering board oversight, public reporting, appeals, donor privacy, spending, reserves and financial reporting.

CandidPlatinum

How openly does it share what it does?

Seal of Transparency levels for sharing information about finances, programs and results. Platinum is the highest.

The questions donors actually ask

Short answers first. Every number links back to the section above that shows the math.

Is Direct Relief a good charity?+

Every major evaluator gives Direct Relief its top mark: a perfect 100 from Charity Navigator, an A+ from CharityWatch, 99% from Forbes, all 20 BBB Wise Giving standards, and Candid's Platinum seal. See the ratings

Why are Direct Relief's ratios so high?+

Most of what Direct Relief spends is donated medicine, recorded at fair value (wholesale acquisition cost for FDA-approved medicines). That pushes the program share to 99.31%. Count cash only and it's about 90% over three years. See both views

Who pays for fundraising?+

Fundraising is paid for by endowment earnings and legacy bequests, not by your gift. In FY2025, fundraising expenses were $3.5M, or 0.15% of total expenses.

Does Direct Relief take government money?+

No. Direct Relief does not accept government funding. It's privately funded by individuals, companies and foundations.

Can I choose where my gift goes?+

Yes. A gift for a specific emergency or program is used only for that purpose, including directly related administrative costs. If that isn't possible, Direct Relief offers your money back. Donation policy

Can I donate medicine I have at home?+

No. Industry regulations prevent Direct Relief from accepting unrequested or individual prescription donations from the public. Medicine comes from manufacturers and licensed distributors.
